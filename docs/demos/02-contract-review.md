# UC-D2 — Contract review, trainee deck, client email

**Film:** [uc-d2-contract-review.mp4](videos/uc-d2-contract-review.mp4)
**Language of the app:** English. **Person:** Priya Nair, solicitor,
Nair & Co, London.

Script: `tests/demo/scenarios/contract-review.ts`.

## The job

16:10. Harbourline’s general counsel sent draft 3 of a master services
agreement, 41 clauses, and wants a position before their board on 8 October.
The firm’s 2026 clause playbook is the standard. Priya already has a note
from Monday’s call: their insurer set liability at £2 million, they will
carve out pre-existing IP, and 60-day payment is their norm — the 90 days
in the draft is a template leftover.

Without the desktop this is a late night: read the draft against the
playbook, keep a risk table somewhere else, then write the client email
and, if there is time, something the trainees can learn from.

## What the desktop does

Project **Harbourline – MSA review**. Skills: `docx`, `xlsx`, `pptx`,
`email-draft`, `calendar-event`. The Monday call note is already a
Markdown file in the project. The draft and the playbook are files in the
project folder; the skills read them on this computer.

1. She opens the call note from the chat, so the source of the client
   context is on screen before she asks.
2. One request: a Word memo with a clause-by-clause table (their wording,
   the playbook position, the risk, a proposed redline) and an Excel risk
   register sorted by severity.
3. The reply comes back with 13 clauses that need attention and 28 that
   are aligned or minor. Three are high: an uncapped confidentiality
   breach against a £2 million insurer position, an IP assignment that
   would sweep in background tools, and 90-day payment against a playbook
   floor of 30. Schedule 2 (service levels) is named as **missing**, not
   invented.
4. A second request turns those three points into a five-slide trainee
   deck: liability caps, assignment versus licence, and the cash-flow
   difference between 30, 45, 60 and 90 days on a £480k contract.
5. A third request writes the cover email as an unsent Outlook draft, with
   the memo and the register named as attachments, and a calendar file for
   the negotiation call on Thursday 1 October, 14:00–15:00.

## Why this is faster

The memo, the register and the trainee deck are three views of one reading,
so a number that changes in the negotiation is changed once and the teaching
material still matches the advice. The email is a draft she sends herself.
The call is a file she drops into her calendar, with the three headlines
already in the description.

## What this film does not claim

The desktop does not file the advice, does not send the email, and does not
decide which redline the firm will actually offer. It also does not replace
a qualified read of a schedule that was never in the bundle.
